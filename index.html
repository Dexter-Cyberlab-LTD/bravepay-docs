1 · BravePay in one page

BravePay, "the bank for the bold", is built so that customers open an account, send and receive money and, as each partner is connected, pay with a card and pay bills, all from one app. Behind that app, four organisations work together. BravePay is in charge of the customer and of every decision; three specialist partners each do one part of the work inside a clearly agreed boundary.

BravePay

The front desk and the manager's office. Every customer request comes to BravePay first. BravePay checks it, decides it, gives it a reference number, sends it to the right partner and follows it until it is finished.

BankOne

The vault and the official ledger book. Run by the technology company Qore, BankOne keeps every BravePay account and balance in the bank's official books.

NIBSS

The national road between banks. Every transfer between two Nigerian banks travels on it, and NIBSS works out what each bank owes the others.

Interswitch

The card network. When a BravePay Verve card is used at a shop or ATM, Interswitch carries the request. It also provides ready-made services such as bill payments.

The one rule to remember

Customers only ever deal with BravePay. BravePay deals with the partners. This keeps the rules, the limits, the security and the records for every customer in one place, BravePay's, whichever partner does the work behind the scenes.

2 · The four organisations, and where each role begins and ends

A good way to picture BravePay is as a bank building. BravePay owns the front desk and the manager's office. Each partner runs one other part of the building under contract, and only that part.

B

BravePay

BravePay Microfinance Bank and BravePay Financial Services App live

What it provides

  • The BravePay app and the business portal, designed to be the only way customers reach their money Planned.
  • Opening accounts and deciding whether to accept each customer.
  • The rules for every payment: PIN, limits, fraud checks and approvals.
  • Choosing which partner carries each payment, and following it to the end.
  • Its own complete record of every request, every answer and every decision Being built.
  • Alerts, customer support, complaints and disputes.
  • Checking every morning that all the records agree Being built.

Where its role begins and ends

Begins: the moment a customer opens the app.

Ends: BravePay does not itself keep the bank's official books (BankOne does), does not run the national transfer road (NIBSS does) and does not run the card network (Interswitch does). It decides and records; the partners carry out.

Everyday comparison: the manager who signs off every request and keeps the master file, even when a courier delivers the parcel.

1

BankOne

Core banking system, run by the technology company Qore Being set up

What it provides

  • Every BravePay customer account, with its 10-digit Nigerian account number.
  • Every balance, and the bank's official record of every movement of money (the "book of record").
  • Moving money between two BravePay accounts.
  • BravePay's connection to other banks for transfers (the exact route is To be confirmed).
  • The connection between the bank and Interswitch's card network.
  • Account controls such as freezing an account when BravePay asks.

Where its role begins and ends

Begins: when BravePay asks it to open an account or move money, and whenever money arrives for a BravePay customer.

Ends: BankOne does not decide who may become a customer or whether a payment may go ahead: BravePay does that before it asks. BankOne's own customer alerts are planned to be switched off, so customers hear only from BravePay Planned.

Not the settlement party: Qore is a technology provider. The real cash between banks moves through the settlement bank at the Central Bank (chapter 14).

Everyday comparison: the vault and the official ledger book, kept by a specialist firm for the bank.

N

NIBSS

Nigeria Inter-Bank Settlement System Being set up, through BankOne

What it provides

  • The national switch that every bank-to-bank transfer in Nigeria crosses.
  • The name check: asking the receiving bank whose account a number belongs to, before money moves.
  • Delivering a transfer to the receiving bank in seconds.
  • Working out, at each settlement session, what each bank owes every other bank.

Where its role begins and ends

Begins: when a payment leaves one bank for another.

Ends: NIBSS carries the instruction and calculates who owes whom. It does not hold customer accounts and does not see BravePay's customers.

Everyday comparison: the national road between banks, with a clerk who adds up every bank's bill at the end of each session.

I

Interswitch

Card network and payment services Agreed, not signed

What it provides

  • Membership of the Verve card scheme, and a range of card numbers for BravePay (whether BankOne supports a range owned by BravePay is To be confirmed).
  • Carrying card payments from any shop till or ATM to BravePay's bank and back.
  • Making and managing cards: blocking, PIN changes.
  • Ready-made services: bill and airtime payments, bulk payouts, collections, and a backup route for transfers.
  • Card fraud monitoring and a dispute portal.

Where its role begins and ends

Begins: when a BravePay card is used, or when BravePay asks it to pay a bill.

Ends: Interswitch checks the card and PIN; BankOne checks the money. Interswitch does not hold BravePay's customer accounts.

Everyday comparison: the card network that reaches every ATM and shop in Nigeria.

Also involved: the settlement bank, the Central Bank and identity checking

Settlement bank and the Central Bank (CBN). Banks pay each other the real cash through accounts at the Central Bank. A microfinance bank like BravePay MFB does this through a commercial "settlement bank". One has been identified; the agreement is not yet signed Planned.

Identity checking. When a customer signs up, BravePay uses licensed identity-checking services to compare their name and date of birth with the national BVN and NIN records. The decision to accept the customer stays with BravePay.

Why it is built this way

Every request a customer makes passes through BravePay. Some things happen without passing through BravePay first, such as card payments and money arriving from other banks, so BravePay also checks customers' accounts for them. That is why it keeps its own complete record of every request and every answer, and checks it against the partners' records every morning Being built.

Two links run directly between partners, because banking requires them: BankOne's connection to NIBSS (for transfers) and the card link between Interswitch and BankOne (for card payments, which need an answer within about two seconds). BravePay requires both to report back to it; the daily records that make this possible are still being arranged with the partners To be confirmed.

4 · Opening an account and proving who you are

Every customer starts here. The aim is simple: make sure the person is who they say they are, open exactly one account for them, and give them a 10-digit account number they can share.

Why name and date of birth must match

Your details are compared with the national BVN or NIN record. If they differ, even by a spelling, the check cannot confirm you. Once confirmed, the details are locked.

Account levels

Each account has a level (a "tier") that sets how much can be held and sent. Higher levels need more proof of identity. BravePay sets and enforces these limits.

One person, one account

If a reply from the bank is delayed, BravePay looks up what already happened instead of asking again, so a second account is never opened by accident.

What BankOne records, and what BravePay records

BankOne records the customer and the account in the bank's official books, and gives the account number. BravePay keeps its own customer record as the master copy, with BankOne's account number stored on it. Either side can be traced to the other at any time.

5 · Signing in and using your account

Signing in

Your BravePay app is linked to your phone. Signing in works like a key card for the building; anything that moves money also needs your transaction PIN, like a signature.

Your balance

The balance you see comes from BankOne, the bank's official books. If BankOne is briefly unavailable, the app shows your last known balance, clearly marked with the time it was taken.

Your history

Your transaction list combines BankOne's official entries with BravePay's own details, such as who you paid and what kind of payment it was.

Your alerts Planned

The plan is that every alert comes from BravePay, one sender with one set of rules, so you are not sent the same message twice by different systems.

What happens when you open the app

The app asks BravePay, never a partner directly. BravePay confirms it is really you on your linked phone, then fetches your balance from BankOne and your history from both BankOne and its own records. No partner ever talks to your phone.

6 · Sending money to another bank

This is the journey BravePay customers make most often. Watch it end to end, then try the two things that can go differently: an answer that does not arrive, and a mistyped account number.

Two parts of every transfer: the message and the cash

A transfer has two separate parts. The message ("pay Mum ₦50,000") travels in seconds, and the receiving bank credits its customer straight away. The real cash between the two banks moves later, at settlement: NIBSS adds up all the transfers between every pair of banks and only the difference is paid, through each bank's settlement bank at the Central Bank.

Why the name appears before you enter your PIN

The name check asks the receiving bank whose account the number belongs to before any money moves. If the name is wrong, you stop, and nothing needs to be reversed. The name always comes through the same route that will carry the payment, so it matches the account the money will reach.

What the fee is

The fee shown before you confirm is BravePay's published fee for the transfer. You see it before you enter your PIN.

7 · Sending money to another BravePay customer

When both people bank with BravePay, the money never leaves the bank, so there is no trip along the national road between banks. BravePay finds the name in its own records, and BankOne moves the money between the two accounts.

8 · Receiving money

Money from another bank arrives through NIBSS, which knows from the account number that it belongs at BravePay's bank. BankOne credits the account in the official books, and BravePay notices the new money and tells the customer.

How fast?

The bank credits the account as soon as the money arrives. The Central Bank's rules for transfers are understood to require this within 4 minutes To be confirmed: the original circular is still being retrieved, and whether it covers the newest NIBSS system is being checked. BravePay's own target is to alert active customers within about 2 minutes of the money landing.

9 · Choosing and using a BravePay card

A bank card lets you pay in shops, online and at cash machines (ATMs) straight from your account. The company that runs the network a card travels on is called the card scheme: it is the name printed on the card, such as Verve, Visa or Mastercard. BravePay's leadership wants customers to be able to choose. No BravePay card can be ordered yet: Verve is the first planned card Agreed, not signed, and Visa and Mastercard have been requested as options, with no agreement in place yet Planned.

How a card payment works

BravePay Verve cards will work at shop card machines and ATMs on the Interswitch network. A card payment is answered in about two seconds, so it travels directly between Interswitch and BankOne. BravePay sets the rules each payment must meet and finds out about the payment afterwards, when it next checks the account; a direct notice from Interswitch has been requested To be confirmed.

Card controls in the app

Customers will be able to block a card, change its PIN and set limits from the BravePay app. A block is sent to Interswitch's card system, with a second block at BankOne as a backup.

PIN privacy

When you set or change a card PIN, it is scrambled on your own phone before it leaves. BravePay passes it on but never sees it.

Who checks what

Interswitch checks the card, the PIN and the card limit; BankOne checks the money To be confirmed. Which system holds the card limit is being confirmed with the partners.

10 · Paying bills and buying airtime

The plan is to pay bills and airtime through Interswitch's Quickteller service, once the agreement is signed Planned. BravePay takes the money from the customer's account first, then pays the biller from BravePay's prepaid balance with Interswitch. Until then, bills use BravePay's existing bill partners.

The refund rule

Money is returned only when a bill payment has definitely failed. If the answer is unclear, BravePay asks again until it is sure, so a customer is never refunded for a bill that was actually paid, and never charged for one that wasn't.

11 · Banking for businesses

Businesses use the BravePay business portal. The same rules apply, with extra controls for money that leaves a business.

Two people for every payout

A bulk payment needs two different approvers, so no single person can move a company's money alone.

Every payment traceable

Each employee's pay is its own payment with its own reference, so any single payment can be traced and explained.

Getting paid Planned

Interswitch's business collection service, including payment links, could let a business's own customers pay it. BravePay would double-check every payment with Interswitch before marking an invoice paid.

Bulk payouts Agreed, not signed

Interswitch's payout service is available for very large runs; it will be used only if volumes justify it.

12 · What happens behind every transaction

BravePay is designed so that every payment, whatever its type, goes through the same five stages Being built.

  1. Verification: is it really you?Your linked phone and your PIN.
  2. Approval: is it allowed?Your account level, daily limit, fraud signals and balance. Business payouts need two approvers.
  3. Recording: write it down firstThe payment gets its own reference number and is written down before anything is sent, and the money is held so it cannot be spent twice.
  4. Processing: one partner, one routeBravePay sends the payment by one route only. It is never sent twice and never switched to a second route while its result is unknown.
  5. Confirmation: a clear answerOnly a clear answer from the partner finishes the payment. Silence never releases the held money.

The three possible endings

Every payment ends in one of three ways: it worked, it definitely failed, or nobody knows yet. The third one is the dangerous one, and it has strict rules. Try each:

A real-life way to picture it: you send a parcel and the courier's phone line drops before they confirm. You don't send a second parcel straight away, because you might pay twice. You call back with the tracking number until they say "delivered" or "returned".

Three levels of proof Being built

BravePay never treats one message as proof that a payment happened. It counts as done only when three levels agree:

1

The partner's answer

BankOne, NIBSS or Interswitch says the payment succeeded.

seconds
2

The entry in the books

The matching entry appears in BankOne's account records. A "success" with no entry is raised as a problem within the hour.

minutes
3

The morning check

BravePay's record, BankOne's books, the NIBSS and Interswitch reports and the settlement bank's statement all agree.

next morning

Like a shop: the cashier says "paid" (proof 1), the receipt is in the till roll (proof 2), and the till balances at closing time (proof 3).

The morning check (reconciliation) Being built

Every morning, BravePay lines up five sets of records for the previous day: its own, BankOne's books, NIBSS's figures, Interswitch's reports and the settlement bank's statement. It ticks off everything that matches. Anything that does not match is given an owner and a deadline, and every unexplained naira is held aside with a case open. Nothing is "fixed" quietly: a correction is a new entry approved by two people, and management receives a summary by 08:00.

13 · When things go wrong

Networks drop, systems slow down, people press Send twice. Three rules keep customers safe: never send the same payment twice, never refund while the result is unknown, and anything unclear goes to a person.

When a partner gives a wrong or late answer

Kemi's ₦9,000 transfer to another BravePay customer is reported as successful, but a later check says it failed. BravePay's check of both accounts shows no matching entries, so a problem is raised the same hour, not the next morning. A case is opened with BankOne with both answers attached; BankOne confirms which is true; Kemi's record is corrected with a new entry (never an edit), she is told plainly, and the fee is waived if BravePay's message was wrong. Without BravePay's own records, a partner's mistake would become a customer complaint with no evidence.

Sorting out a disputed payment

Card disputes and the backup transfer route

Disputes about card payments follow the Verve card scheme's rules; their time limits are not yet documented and are being confirmed To be confirmed. Disputes about payments sent on Interswitch's backup transfer route are handled on Interswitch's dispute portal, where the receiving bank has 48 hours to respond.

14 · Where the money actually is

A customer sees one balance. Behind it, money sits in a few different places, each with its own keeper and its own check. Watch how the money moves in the four journeys customers make most often:

Chidi sends ₦50,000 to his mum at GTBank

Taken from Chidi's account at BankOne (plus the fee) → NIBSS records that BravePay owes GTBank ₦50,000 → GTBank credits Mum in seconds → the banks settle the cash later, at 02:00 or 14:00, through their settlement banks.

Musa's ₦185,000 salary arrives

Taken from the employer's account at another bank → NIBSS routes it to the bank holding Musa's account → BankOne credits Musa → BravePay notices and says "Money received" → the employer's bank pays BravePay's settlement bank at settlement.

Ngozi pays ₦18,500 at a shop with her card Agreed, not signed

Taken from Ngozi's account at the moment of approval → Interswitch records the card payment → the next working day, BravePay's settlement bank pays the shop's bank, which pays the shop.

Kemi pays her ₦9,000 DStv bill Agreed, not signed

Taken from Kemi's account into BravePay's bills account → DStv is paid from BravePay's prepaid balance with Interswitch → BravePay's finance team tops that balance up from the bills account.

16 · How customers and their money are kept safe

Every connection between two organisations is locked, and each lock answers two questions: is this really who it says it is, and can anyone read or change the message on the way?

Your phone and BravePay

A key card for the building (your linked phone), plus a signature (your PIN) for anything that moves money.

BravePay and BankOne

A key that only works from BravePay's own front door. Only BravePay holds it, and BravePay checks every customer before using it.

BravePay and Interswitch

A visitor badge that expires and has to be renewed with BravePay's ID card.

Interswitch telling BravePay

A sealed envelope: if the seal is wrong, the message is binned, and even a correct one is double-checked.

Between the partners

The links between BankOne, NIBSS and Interswitch run on private connections, and each partner secures its own side under contract.

Cards at a shop or ATM

The PIN is checked inside Interswitch's secure equipment, never by BravePay.

Other protections

  • Card details. BravePay's rule before cards launch: full card numbers, card security codes and PINs are never stored by BravePay.
  • Staff. Administrators join by invitation only Live. Role-based staff tools with two-step sign-in, and two-person approval for corrections, refunds, manual credits and route changes Planned.
  • A permanent record Being built. Every request, answer, decision and staff action is written to a record that cannot be edited; corrections are new entries pointing at the old ones. Records are to be kept for 7 years Planned.
  • Customer data. Each partner receives only the customer details its job needs. Identity numbers and phone numbers are hidden in BravePay's logs Live.
  • Partners never reach in. No partner can reach BravePay's core systems. They can reach only a small number of locked doors, and everything they send is checked before it is acted on.

17 · The value BravePay delivers

For customers

  • One app for everything, with one set of rules.
  • The receiver's name shown before the PIN, so mistakes are caught before money moves.
  • Never charged twice: an unclear payment is held, followed up and never re-sent.
  • Money never released or refunded on a guess; always on a clear answer.
  • Alerts from one sender (planned), and a balance that is honest about its time when a partner is down.
  • Complaints handled with every piece of evidence on one screen.
  • Card controls and card PIN privacy in the app (with cards).

For businesses

  • Salary and supplier runs with two-person approval.
  • Every individual payment traceable by its own reference.
  • Failures listed clearly, never re-sent behind your back.
  • Collections and payment links through Interswitch (planned).
  • Records checked against the partners' every morning.

For financial partners

  • Every customer and every payment is checked by BravePay before it reaches a partner.
  • One route per type of payment, so a payment is never sent through two partners at once.
  • Clear, written boundaries: each partner knows exactly which part of the work is theirs.
  • A daily match of BravePay's records against each partner's reports, with every difference owned and explained.
  • Complete, traceable records for disputes and regulatory examinations.
